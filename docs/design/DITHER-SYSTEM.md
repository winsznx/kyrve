# The Encrypted Field

Kyrve's visual primitive: the complete private capital curve, drawn as a field of unreadable data.

Generated by `pnpm design:dither` → `scripts/design/build-dither-assets.py`.
Rendered by `apps/web/src/components/EncryptedField.tsx`.
Hashes and byte costs in [`dither-manifest.json`](dither-manifest.json).

---

## What it is

A field of small marks whose **density** carries the shape of the curve while no individual mark
carries a value. Layered curve silhouettes, rendered as dots and short fragments in Ash on Onyx, with
density varying by how sharply the curve bends — which is where a real book carries its information.

It is the product's thesis as a picture: a book exists, it has structure, and you cannot read it.

## What it is not

- **Not a chart.** No axis, no label, no tick, no legend.
- **Not a redaction.** Nothing in it is derived from data of any kind. The density function is
  geometric, seeded at `0x4B59525645`. There is no number behind it to recover, which is a stronger
  statement than "the numbers are hidden".
- **Not a blur.** A blur implies the values are present in the page and merely obscured.
- **Not noise.** Random marks would carry no shape; these carry the curve and nothing else.
- **Not a network graphic.** No nodes, no edges, no orbiting particles.

## The Cobalt point

One point, drawn in Cobalt, **only when a quote is genuinely public**.

It is never baked into a raster. It is an SVG overlay drawn at runtime by `EncryptedField` when
`resolved` is true, because an image with the point compiled in would show it on every page that used
that image — including pages where no quote exists. The mark means *exactly one thing became public*,
and it must never claim that falsely.

This is also the reason the mechanism sequence works: three fields at increasing resolution, and the
Cobalt mark appears only on the third.

## Where it is used

| Surface | Field | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Landing hero | `hero` | 0.0 — the whole book |
| Mechanism step 1 | `mechanism-1` | 0.0 |
| Mechanism step 2 | `mechanism-2` | 0.45 |
| Mechanism step 3 | `mechanism-3` | 0.92, with the Cobalt point |
| Final call to action | `close` | 0.85, with the Cobalt point |
| Private matching page | `matching` | 0.35 |
| Empty states that need a visual | `empty` | 0.0 |
| Onboarding background | `start` (reuses `empty`) | 0.0 |

**Not behind every card and not on every page.** It is atmospheric where the argument is being made
and absent where somebody is doing work — a field behind a form is decoration competing with a task.

## Why a build step

A field dense enough to read as data is tens of thousands of marks. As runtime SVG that is hundreds
of kilobytes of DOM and a layout cost on every route; as a canvas it is a paint loop on a phone for a
picture that never changes. Neither is the right trade for an image identical on every load.

So it is generated deterministically and shipped as a raster, with the live point as a tiny SVG
overlay. Same seed, same curve geometry as the live component — one object, two renderers.

## Delivery

- **AVIF primary, WebP fallback, PNG last.** Quality steps down until the budget is met and stops;
  it never drops below the floor, and the manifest records an over-budget asset rather than shipping
  a ruined one.
- **Explicit `width` and `height`** on every image, so the box is reserved before the bytes land and
  nothing shifts.
- **Mobile derivatives at 900px**, resampled from the full render rather than regenerated — a second
  generation at a smaller size produces a different mark distribution, and the two would visibly
  disagree at a breakpoint.
- **`loading="lazy"`** everywhere except the hero, which is `eager` with `fetchPriority="high"`.
- **No alpha channel.** A transparent edge is where halos come from when a field lands on Graphite;
  the raster is opaque Onyx and a 0.5% scale hides the resampling seam.

Every field is inside its budget at the top quality step, so nothing was degraded to hit a number.
Mobile derivatives are held to one third of the same budget.

## Motion

The field does not animate. It is a still image, and the only motion near it is the section entry and
the tagline reveal — both on one easing curve, both with a `prefers-reduced-motion` path that renders
the finished state rather than a zero-duration version of the animation.
